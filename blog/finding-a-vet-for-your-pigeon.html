Finding a Vet for Your Pigeon

October 6, 2026
Marilynn Walz

You can do everything perfectly in your care, day in and day out. Accidents can happen. Emergencies can happen. If you're unprepared and rushing to find a vet at the last second, it could be too late for your bird, whether it's a broken bone, heavy bleeding, egg binding, or so much more.

Keep in mind that some avian veterinarians may not have the necessary expertise to properly diagnose or provide appropriate care advice for your pet pigeon. Things like dietary or enclosure recommendations are very different from parrots. Even something simple like fenbendazole, which is commonly prescribed for parrots as a dewormer, can be extremely toxic and kill your pigeon.

We recommend visiting the Association of Avian Veterinarians website and searching for a vet in your area. It's also a good idea to ask in the Palomacy Help Group on Facebook if anyone knows any vets in your area who are trustworthy. Owners, rehabbers, or rescues may be able to refer you to one.


Screening A Vet

If you got your pigeon from a rescue or shelter or from a local person, see if they know any reputable veterinarians in your area. Many larger rescues have adopters who may live near you and can point you to a pigeon-friendly veterinarian.

Screen your veterinarian beforehand to get an idea of their experience.

  • How often do you see pigeons? Are they pets? Any vet that doesn't see birds, let alone pigeons, is a no. Additionally, some veterinarians don't see pet pigeons. Similar to vets who mostly see livestock, holistic methods can be "prescribed" when they do more harm than good by avoiding direct treatment.
  • What's your recommended diet for pet pigeons? Pigeons should be on a seed-based diet, or one that's made of a complete pellet. Additionally, they need a multivitamin and soluble grit for all their nutritional needs. Vegetables and fruits should never be the staple of any pigeon's diet.
  • What do you give to deworm pigeons? Fenbendazole, also known as Panacur, is a common dewormer for parrots; it is toxic to pigeons.
  • What preventative schedule would you recommend? This can include testing for specific parasites and illnesses on an annual or bi-annual basis. Vaccinations for pigeons are hard to find and not practical for most clinics.

Red Flags

  • Claiming pigeons need to be on a veggie-based diet. Pigeons are naturally granivores!
  • Prescribing or recommending fenbendazole (Panacur)
  • Saying to "wait and see" for health issue progression. Pigeons hide illness very well, and serious concerns can be noticed too late.

Is there financial assistance for pet pigeons?

There's only one company that provides insurance for pet pigeons: Nationwide Pet Insurance.

I recommend just setting funds aside in a savings account for emergencies and throwing in what you can when you can. If you can't get insurance, GoFundMe has worked wonders for pet owners, but it's very unreliable and takes donations to go through.
